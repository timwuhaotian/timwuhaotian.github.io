Tap

A small, fast, quiet browser for the Mac, written in Rust.

Pages run on WebKit, the engine already inside every Mac (Safari's), so there is no second browser engine to download, update and keep in memory. Tap is a Rust port of Search, an MIT-licensed Swift browser: Search's behaviour is the specification, rewritten with AppKit and WebKit through objc2, under its own name and icon. It browses well enough to be your Mac's browser, and it updates itself. (Status: early — not all of Search is in yet.)

Highlights

One field, your tabs, and nothing else.

Tap keeps Search's shape: one field that takes addresses and searches, tabs across the top with the traffic lights in the row, and an address bar that gets out of the way.

One field, two jobs

Addresses and searches in a single field. It suggests your visited pages by host, path or query, with a grey ending after the caret when it can finish an address — Tab or → takes it. Pinyin and kana are left alone while an input method is composing.

Tabs across the top

⌘T, ⌘W, ⇥⌘T, ⌃Tab and ⌘1–⌘9, with the traffic lights in the tab row. Links open behind the page with ⌘-click, and each tab carries its site's icon — including sites whose only icon is an SVG.

Tab groups in colour

A group's name heads its tabs on a chip of its colour, right-click it to choose another, and a click folds the group away or opens it again. Right-click a tab to move it into a group, rename, duplicate, sleep it or reopen a closed one.

An address bar that tells the truth

Back, forward and reload on its left, the page's address, a lock or a “Not secure” warning, the certificate a click away, and the load's progress as a line that glides forward and fades once the page is in. Settings can hide it.

Coding agents browse with it

Tap puts a tap command in ~/.local/bin and a skill where Claude Code, Codex, Gemini and opencode look: tap open, tap snapshot (each control with a ref, shadow roots and iframes included), tap click, tap fill, tap press, tap text, tap eval, tap console.

Nothing phones home

No account, no sync, no telemetry. The only requests Tap makes on its own are the pages you open, their icons, and a look at this repository's releases/latest.toml every six hours for an update — which Settings › About turns off.

Get it

One DMG, no second engine.

Tap ships as a signed and notarized disk image straight out of the repository's releases folder — there are no GitHub Release attachments to hunt for.

Download

Grab the disk image and drop Tap in Applications:

  • Tap-arm64.dmg — the only build, ~1.6 MB
  • macOS 14 or later, Apple silicon
  • Signed and notarized by Apple
  • Current build 0.3.4 (releases/latest.toml); Tap-arm64.dmg.sha256 sits beside it

Direct link

The raw DMG from the repo's releases folder:

https://github.com/timwuhaotian/tap/raw/main/releases/Tap-arm64.dmg

Build from source

You need a Mac with Apple silicon, Xcode, and Rust 1.94 or later. A build you make yourself is signed ad hoc, so it runs only on your own Mac.

cargo run -p tap-app # debug build, straight from the tree scripts/bundle.sh debug # or a Tap.app in target/debug cargo xtask icon # redraw icon PNGs from packaging/icon.svg
